# The C_clip problem

## Context (for motivation only — the actual task is below)

We are computing $P = \mathbb{P}(x^4+bx^3+cx^2+dx+e \text{ has 4 real roots})$
for $(b,c,d,e)$ i.i.d. uniform on $[-1,1]$. Through an exact reduction (verified
independently four separate ways), $16P$ splits as

$$16P = U - C_{\text{clip}}, \qquad U = \frac{\sqrt3\,\operatorname{asinh}(\sqrt6/4)}{90} + \frac{7013\sqrt{11}}{302400} = 0.088066959660626860659\ldots$$

$U$ (99.28% of the answer) is fully established in closed form. **$C_{\text{clip}}$
is the one remaining piece, and no closed form has been found for it despite a
real search** (details at the end). This is the actual task: find a closed form
for $C_{\text{clip}}$, or a rigorous argument that none exists in reasonable terms.

## Exact definition of $C_{\text{clip}}$

Define, for $b\in[-1,1]$, $m\in(0, M(b)]$ with $M(b) := \dfrac{3b^2+8}{16}$, and
$\tau\in(-\tau_c,\tau_c)$ with $\tau_c := \dfrac{8}{3\sqrt3}$:

$$\Lambda(\tau) := S\,(4-3S^2)^{3/2}, \qquad \text{where } S\in[1,\,2/\sqrt3] \text{ solves } \tau = 4S(S^2-1) \text{ for } \tau\ge0,$$

and $\Lambda(-\tau) := \Lambda(\tau)$ ($\Lambda$ is even). Note $\Lambda(0)=1$,
$\Lambda(\pm\tau_c)=0$, $\int_{-\tau_c}^{\tau_c}\Lambda(\tau)\,d\tau = \frac{128}{105}$
exactly.

Also define

$$d(b,m,\tau) := \tau\, m^{3/2} + \frac{b^3}{16} - bm.$$

Then:

$$C_{\text{clip}} \;=\; \int_{-1}^{1}\int_{0}^{M(b)}\int_{-\tau_c}^{\tau_c} m^{7/2}\,\Lambda(\tau)\;\mathbf{1}\bigl[\,|d(b,m,\tau)| > 1\,\bigr]\;d\tau\,dm\,db$$

**Target numeric value** (established to ~41 digits, cross-checked by
independent Monte Carlo to 6+ significant digits):

$$C_{\text{clip}} = 0.000637674211187091936250935532440028016216419\ldots$$

## Known structural facts that may help

- The clipping region $\{|d|>1\}$ is nonempty only near $b \approx -0.614$. The
  onset (where $|d|=1$ first touches the domain, i.e. where the maximum of
  $|d(b,m,\tau)|$ over $(m,\tau)$ at fixed $b$ equals exactly $1$) occurs at the
  **real root of an irreducible cubic**:
  $$27b^3 - 9b^2 + 108b + 76 = 0, \qquad b^\* = -0.61430210141629608275214786\ldots$$
  (this is *not* a free parameter — it is forced by the geometry, and its
  appearance in closed form is basically guaranteed to be part of any exact
  expression for $C_{\text{clip}}$).
- $d(b,m,\tau)$ is linear in $\tau$ at fixed $(b,m)$, so for fixed $(b,m)$ the
  constraint $|d|>1$ is a union of at most two half-lines in $\tau$ — i.e. the
  inner $\tau$-integral, for fixed $(b,m)$, is in principle expressible via the
  antiderivative of $\Lambda$ (which is elementary, since $\Lambda$ came from
  the closed form of $K$ above) evaluated at the (generally irrational, $m$- and
  $b$-dependent) crossing point(s) of $|d|=1$. That is: the *inner* integral is
  elementary for fixed $(b,m)$; the difficulty is doing the remaining
  $(b,m)$-integral of that closed-but-messy inner result.
- This mirrors the situation in a related, already-solved problem (a cubic
  polynomial analogue): there, integrating the "wrong" variable first left an
  integral that provably has no elementary closed form (confirmed by a blind
  PSLQ search finding nothing at 40 digits for several sample values), while
  integrating a *different* variable first (specifically: the variable that
  entered the domain boundary only through a simple algebraic combination)
  made everything elementary. **The analogous question here is whether there
  is a better order of integration than $(\tau, m, b)$** — e.g. integrating $b$
  first at fixed $(m,\tau)$, or a substitution that straightens the cubic
  $27b^3-9b^2+108b+76=0$ (e.g. depressing it, or substituting
  $b = b^\* + $ something).

## What has already been tried on $C_{\text{clip}}$ (do not just repeat this)

PSLQ / integer-relation search at 41-digit precision, against a
**structure-informed** 25-constant basis: $\sqrt2,\sqrt3,\sqrt6,\sqrt{11},
\sqrt{22},\sqrt{33}$, $\pi$, $\log 2$, $\log 3$, $\operatorname{asinh}(\sqrt6/4)$,
$\sqrt3\cdot\operatorname{asinh}(\sqrt6/4)$, the cubic irrational $b^\*$ and
$b^{*2}$, $u_0 := \sqrt{3b^{*2}+8}$ and $u_0^{3,5,7,9}$, $b^\* u_0^{3,5,7,9}$,
$A_0 := \operatorname{asinh}(b^\*\sqrt3/(2\sqrt2))$, $\sqrt3\cdot A_0$ — at
1, 2, 3-term PSLQ with coefficient bounds up to $10^8$. **Nothing found.** This
was a narrower search than ideal (only 3 terms, and the basis is a guess at
the right algebraic field generated by $b^\*$), so a negative here is much
weaker evidence than for $U$ — there is real room for a different model to
find something this search missed, especially by reasoning about the correct
field first (e.g. what does $\mathbb{Q}(b^\*)$ actually look like, given the
minimal polynomial above?) rather than guessing a constant pool.

## What a good answer looks like

Either:
(a) An explicit closed form for $C_{\text{clip}}$ in elementary functions,
    logs/arcsinh/arctan of algebraic quantities in $b^\*$ — verifiable against
    the 41-digit value above; or
(b) A concrete, checkable reduction of the $b$-integral to a *single*
    one-dimensional integral in closed-form-except-for-one-transcendental-step
    (e.g. "$C_{\text{clip}} = \int_{b_1}^{b_2} F(b)\,db$ with $F$ elementary,
    reducing the problem to whether $\int F$ has an elementary antiderivative"
    — this would still be real progress even without finishing it); or
(c) A well-argued case for why no elementary closed form should be expected
    (e.g. via differential Galois theory / Liouville's theorem reasoning about
    the specific algebraic integrand), which would let us stop searching.
